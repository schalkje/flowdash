Hazard Tape — variations.

Five different levers pulled on the same idea: motion (marching stripes), palette (industrial yellow/black), placement (a horizontal police line across the body), geometry (an X-cross sealing the edge), and direction (chevron arrows replacing parallel stripes). Each is shown on the input (pre) and output (post) edge. Original Hazard Tape sits at the foot for comparison.

No.01

Marching Stripes

lever · motion

The original tape, but the stripes drift along the diagonal at a slow conveyor pace. Adds urgency without changing footprint — the same barricade, but visibly active. Reads from across the room like a working alarm rather than a printed sticker.

motion
stripe drift · 0.9s loop
strength
adds urgency without footprint change
caveat
visual "noise" across many failing nodes
No.02

Industrial Black

lever · palette

Same shape, different palette: yellow on black like a factory-floor caution sign or radiation door. Less "wound", more "do not operate". Pairs well with a graphite/dark dashboard theme. The red nose remains as the only true-red element, so it still carries the failure signal.

palette
yellow on black · red disc preserved
tone
"do not operate" · less wound, more lockout
caveat
less alarming if used alongside dark-theme UI
No.03

Police Line

lever · placement + text

A horizontal yellow-and-black strip slapped across half the node, slightly skewed, with repeating "PRE/POST VALIDATION FAILED" text. The most literal of the set — anyone in the room can read what's wrong without zooming. Costs you the lower half of the node's content area.

placement
horizontal · half-node · −4°
strength
literal label · no hover needed
caveat
overlaps content · loud at scale
No.04

X — Cross

lever · geometry

Two short strips of striped tape cross over the failing edge, like a door taped shut. Doesn't barricade the side — it condemns it. Strongest "this is finished, not in progress" reading. Compact footprint, no narrowing of usable node width.

geometry
two strips · ±38° · centred on nose
tone
condemnation, not warning
caveat
protrudes outside node bounds
No.05

Chevron Bar

lever · direction

Stripes give way to arrows pointing the way data should flow — toward the node on the input edge, away from the node on the output edge. Same vertical band footprint as the classic, but now the indicator also narrates the failure: "this was supposed to come in here" / "this was supposed to go out there".

shape
5 chevrons · alternating yellow / red
strength
indicator narrates the failure direction
caveat
L-to-R flow assumed · breaks on RTL graphs

Classic · vertical band, static stripes, yellow on red. The point of departure for the five variations above.
